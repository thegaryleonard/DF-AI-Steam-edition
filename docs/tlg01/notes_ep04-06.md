# Twisted Logic – Panama Canal Ep04–06: layout-relevant notes

Sources: Ep04 = p58YDa1Kq5E, Ep05 = K2-dH5doArk, Ep06 = TpggB_I31C4. Times are [m:ss] from the auto-captions; quotes are paraphrased where the captions are garbled.
Context: these episodes are mid-game (years 151–152). The population grows from about 30 to 49, with a cap of 50. Most of the time goes to water engineering (the river, ocean drains, water wheels). Typical "first-build" items (entrance, depot) are only touched in passing.

## Entrance / depot / defense
- Ep04 26:37–26:58: stonefall traps in the entrance corridor: "these stonefall traps are like minimal… this really should be longer". He prefers a **longer trap corridor**, lined with stone-fall traps, and says the fort is "not safe at all".
- Ep04 19:11: "we should really move that Workshop — set levers for that main door". Levers for the main door/bridge sit in a separate lever room, not inside a workshop area.
- Ep05/Ep06: the trade depot is in use for elves and dwarves (Ep05 0:13, 74:06; Ep06 1:49). He requests the broker, then moves goods in **bins** by "highest price". Cheap rock mugs and crafts are made in bulk as trade goods (Ep04 10:56: 75 rock mugs; Ep06 85:05: rock crafts 500 when non-economic rock > 100). The depot's location isn't shown.
- Ep06 44:07–44:18: "I always make the bridges so it'll smash things on the other side of the fortress and not on the inside". Raising bridges open/raise **toward the outside**.
- Ep06 41:29–42:24: a non-retracting raising bridge only blocks the one z-level it's on ("it's not a multi Z level device").
- Ep05 47:36: "I really don't like one tile bridges, let's do a two-tile bridge".
- Ep04 57:01–57:38 and Ep06 14:51: ghosts and unhappy dwarves can wreck machines. He once set up a justice system and **jail** after a dwarf broke a pump, so redundant gear assemblies/axles are an option.
- Ep04 (much of it), Ep05 2:50: unsafe water areas get **restricted-area** traffic zones (a whole level marked restricted), locked/forbidden doors, ramps removed so dwarves can't wander into the river, and grates/hatches over openings.

## Staircase / corridors / traffic
- Ep05 15:03: "I just built the stairs a little too close to these two rooms. The stairs really should be one or two [tiles] back so that way we can wall it off". Keep a 1–2 tile gap/landing between a stair and the rooms next to it.
- Ep05 8:17–8:30: at the bottom of a long exploratory stair he digs a **landing area plus a door** "in case it really starts flooding… the water has room to spread out".
- Ep06 17:32–17:42: "When the dwarves have to dodge around each other, like if the hallways are too tight… messes you up. You really don't want that." He widens hallways; the implied rule is that corridors should be wider than 1 tile.
- Ep04 49:05–49:18: he lays out from a **center line**: "the center line and we'll space it out two over". In Ep06 87:45 he aligns the dining-hall furniture on the N–S center line.
- Ep06 10:36–11:06: on the old "stairs with walls/statues in the center" FPS pattern: "I don't know if that's even necessary anymore… never really thought it was necessary". He builds gigantic forts and accepts low FPS.
- Ep06 59:56–68:11: **FPS**: "The less mud the better because plants grow in the mud. Plants growing uses CPU resources". Avoid unnecessary mud and indoor grass.
- Ep06 67:17–67:29: a door placed diagonally to a passage "will still work out fine" (dwarves path diagonally through doors).

## Room sizes / shapes (general rules)
- Ep06 22:48–22:56: "**The minimum I do is a 3 by 3 room**. I don't like to do anything smaller… dwarfs really like the 3 by 3s."
- Ep06 24:14–24:25: "I really don't like rooms that are four spaces or two spaces or even numbers. **I really like the odd numbers**". Ep06 45:10: "I hate four by four".
- Ep06 18:13–18:32: a candidate dining room/guildhall is **11x11**, with another 11x11 beside it ("maybe these will be guild halls instead"). Where the measurements were off, rooms got a **double wall** between them (19:07).
- Ep05 46:43, 57:08: **5x5** rooms for stone-crafter/stoneworker workshops (one per room, stacked over the matching rooms on the lower level), and a 5x5 lever "master control" room.
- Ep04 1:02–1:42: farms are **5x5 plots**. "These two… are going to be plenty of food for the entire Fortress" (about 30–50 dwarves). Extra plots are for cloth (fiber).

- Ep04 49:26, 69:14–72:08: a dedicated **artifact stockpile**: armor, finished goods, furniture, weapons and ammo, artifact quality only, **no bins**. Artifacts are disabled on all other piles. It's next to the main hall; Ep05 89:37 adds a door.

## Water / power (layout-relevant parts only)
- The river is fed by a U-pipe. He adds a lever-linked hatch valve on the intake, grates, and a drain with 14 minecarts on north-dump track stops (Ep04, Ep05 28:56–29:19). Water wheels go 3 tiles apart with a gear assembly in the center and floors for access (Ep04 54:42–55:21; Ep06 12:29–14:12). Wheels only spin if the water flows **off the map** (Ep05 34:00–36:48, 50:52).
- Ep05 46:07: "when you're doing water tubes, you want as few turns as possible".
- Ep05 57:08–58:04: a **5x5 lever "master control" room**. It sits off the main path because the levers don't need quick access.
- Ep06 66:36–67:50: there's no pressure relief on U-pipes, so he adds doors/walls so that a flooded room can't flood the fort. Overflow is routed to a grate → minecart drain.
- Ep05 63:17: "everything in this Fortress is magma safe" (obsidian mechanisms).

## Labor organisation (affects df-ai worker counts)
- Ep05 33:39–35:13, Ep06 35:12–36:03: about 15 stewards who also cut wood; everyone else is a miner plus builder. "At a certain point everybody's going to be a miner." Military with pick/axe uniforms lets them keep mining/woodcutting.
- No barracks or military built in Ep04–06 ("we don't have a military setup", Ep05 35:19).

## Don't-do / warnings
- Don't put stairs flush against rooms; leave 1–2 tiles (Ep05 15:03).
- Avoid tight one-tile hallways (Ep06 17:32).
- No rooms under 3x3, and avoid even dimensions (Ep06 22:48, 24:14).
- Don't overfill stockpiles with furniture; keep work orders at low thresholds (Ep06 31:38).
- Avoid mud and grass indoors for FPS (Ep06 68:02).
- Don't neglect noble bedrooms (Ep06 30:29).
- Short trap corridors are inadequate (Ep04 26:46).
- Don't build workshops in hallways permanently (Ep06 51:21).
- Single-tile bridges: avoid them (Ep05 47:36).

## Build order / priorities observed (Ep04–06, years 151–152)
1. (Already present from earlier episodes) a top-level start area with temporary workshops in the hallways, a catch-all stockpile, a food stockpile, a dormitory/beds, 2× 5x5 farms, a trade depot, a stonefall-trap entrance and a legendary dining hall.
2. Ep04: secure the water source first (hatch valve, grates, restricted zones). Build a furniture stockpile around the carpentry shop, 2–3 mechanic shops and a lever room, finish the entrance stone traps, then set up the artifact stockpile.
3. Ep04 end / Ep05 start: water wheels, then a tomb area for the first death (6x9 memorial plus a peasant tomb-row plan). The craftsdwarf workshops (×4) move into a dedicated area, with 5x5 stoneworker workshop rooms.
4. Ep05: guildhall (pump operator) as a meeting area, ocean drains, a 5x5 lever master room, and labor assignment for migrants (pop 41→49). Carpentry (×3) moves next to the wood pile, and he plans cloth/leather and finished-goods piles deeper in the fort.
5. Ep06 (pop 49): **bedroom block** of about 50 3x3 rooms (door, bed, cabinet, coffer, statue), the noble's big central bedroom, and the dining hall table rows. He clears the top-level temporary workshops, stockpiles and beds, and adds an indoor animal pasture, a bone/refuse pile behind the craft area, a lesser tomb row (28 tombs), guildhall statues, and work orders for furniture with low thresholds.
6. Still pending: noble office, dining room and tomb; hospital; engineering and cooks' guilds; glass shop and sand zone (for the pump stack); a swim trainer; military/barracks.
