# Blind DF tutorial notes (set B): locations, services, nobles, justice

Source: Blind, "Dwarf Fortress - Complete Tutorial/Guide Compilation" (Steam v50+). Timestamps are from the compilation.
Tags: **[BLUEPRINT]** = room layout, furniture, sizes, counts, stockpiles, stock targets, build priority (df-ai JSON). **[BEHAVIOUR]** = zone/location settings, noble assignment, jobs and orders, trading, military, justice logic (df-ai C++).

---

## Taverns / inn / dining hall (2:37–2:44)
- [BLUEPRINT] Furniture to stock: stone mugs (craftsdwarf) or wooden cups, tables and thrones/chairs (stoneworker or carpenter), beds for inn rooms, coffers/chests for mugs and instruments. (2:38:01–2:39:06)
- [BLUEPRINT] **Mugs/cups/goblets are required** for the tavern to work. They are stored in a coffer/chest inside the tavern. Stock target: keep spare mugs. (2:38:18, 2:40:19)
- [BLUEPRINT] **One chair/throne per table, no more.** Extra chairs at one table make dwarves fight over the seat and get bad thoughts. (2:41:22)
- [BLUEPRINT] Put at least one coffer/chest in the tavern. Once it's placed, mugs fill it automatically. (2:41:35)
- [BLUEPRINT] Add a drink stockpile in or next to the tavern (custom > food > drinks; plant drinks only if there's no meat/animal drink). It fills with barrels. (2:41:49)
- [BLUEPRINT] Leave open floor for a dance floor, **at least 6x6** so the largest dances can happen. (2:42:30)
- [BLUEPRINT] Smooth the floor before placing furniture (small value boost). Brick or boulder floors are prettier. (2:41:07)
- [BLUEPRINT] Inn rooms are bedrooms with a bed each, next to the tavern. Long-term residents sleep there. (2:42:40)
- [BLUEPRINT] Small separate dining hall with tables and chairs, next to the tavern. (2:43:28, 2:44:02)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Draw the zone as a meeting area that covers the walls, then use the shield "+" to add a location and pick Inn/Tavern. (2:39:14–2:39:37)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Visitor setting: all visitors / visitors + long-term residents / **citizens only**. Citizens only avoids vampires, werecreatures and thieves. A safe default for an AI. (2:39:48–2:40:11)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Set the location's goblet and instrument target counts. Buy instruments from traders, because they're procedurally generated. (2:40:19, 2:40:49)
- [BEHAVIOUR] **Assign a tavern keeper**, the only dwarf that can use the stored mugs to serve drinks. Prefer a dwarf with skill, or one who already likes hanging out in the tavern. (2:40:28, 2:42:06)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Assign the inn bedroom zones to the tavern location: multi-select, then "+" > assign to tavern, BEFORE dwarves claim them. (2:42:55–2:43:04)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Attach the dining-hall zone to the tavern location. That extends the dance floor, adds socializing, and lets visitors use it. Optional; the hall also works on its own. (2:43:36–2:43:58)

## Tombs / memorial slabs (2:44–2:47)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Bury the dead quickly, otherwise ghosts haunt and hurt living dwarves. (2:44:38)
- [BLUEPRINT] Tomb = a small 1-tile niche dug into a wall underground (or inside a building), with a **door** in front and a **coffin/sarcophagus** inside. (2:45:15–2:45:27)
- [BLUEPRINT] Several niche tombs in a row, each with its own door and coffin, can all be zoned with one drag. The "multi" tomb tool detects them by their doors. Keep a stock of coffins. (2:45:32, 2:45:53)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Zone > Tomb over each niche. Draw individual tombs, or use the multi-tomb drag. A dwarf gets buried automatically. (2:45:41–2:46:08)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Use a slab only when the body is unrecoverable (burned, lost, unreachable). Make a slab at the stoneworker, use its "engrave memorial" task for the specific dead dwarf, then build the slab near the tombs and pick the right one from the list. Memorializing prevents a ghost. (2:44:54, 2:46:13–2:46:59)

## Bedrooms (2:47–2:53)
- [BLUEPRINT] Ideal bedroom = **bed + cabinet (clothes) + chest/coffer (items/toys)**. A weapon rack and armor stand are optional extras. (2:48:10–2:48:23)
- [BLUEPRINT] Simple square rooms with 1 door tile. A corner door works too, because dwarves move diagonally. (2:48:43–2:48:54)
- [BLUEPRINT] Don't let furniture block doorways or the walking path. Diagonal access into a bed is fine. (2:51:41–2:51:46)
- [BLUEPRINT] Build stone doors (stoneworker) for every bedroom. (2:51:56)
- [BLUEPRINT] Build order: dig, then **smooth the floor BEFORE placing furniture**, or the smoothing skips the tiles under it, then furniture, then doors, then the zone. (2:50:39–2:50:48)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Smoothing priority can starve mining. Raise the dig priority to finish the rooms. (2:49:44)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Zone > Bedroom with the "multi" option: one rectangle over a block of furnished rooms makes one bedroom zone per room. Rooms without a bed are skipped. Dwarves then assign themselves, so you don't need to assign per dwarf. (2:52:22–2:53:25)
- [BLUEPRINT] A dormitory zone is the cheaper fallback, but dwarves prefer individual bedrooms. (2:47:39)

## Temples (2:53–2:58)
- [BLUEPRINT] **No furniture is required.** A temple can be an open area, even outdoors. Any size works. (2:53:51, 2:55:04–2:55:08)
- [BLUEPRINT] Recommended: an enclosed room with a door, smoothed, plus an **altar ("offering place")** built from stone or wood. Optional: instruments, chests/coffers, statues, chairs and tables. (2:55:15, 2:55:35–2:56:00, 2:57:38)
- [BLUEPRINT] One shared temple instead of one per god. Per-god temples are cumbersome and confuse pathing and prayer. (2:54:38, 2:57:01–2:57:14)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Meeting-area zone > "+" > new Temple > **no specific deity**. Add deity temples later only when citizens petition for them. (2:56:23–2:57:14, 2:54:42)
- [BEHAVIOUR] A temple you build unprompted has **no value requirement**. Petition-requested temples do. (2:56:11–2:56:23)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Access: citizens + long-term residents (residents such as mercenaries can worship). (2:57:23–2:57:28)

## Mayor / noble offices (2:58–3:02)
- [BEHAVIOUR] The mayor is elected at **50 dwarves**. The noble screen shows the room requirements: red = missing, yellow = partly met. (2:58:40, 2:59:07)
- [BLUEPRINT] Per noble: **3 rooms**, bedroom (quarters) + office (study) + dining room. Separate rooms are preferred, but zones can overlap in one big room. Needs a roof. (2:58:20–2:58:58)
- [BLUEPRINT] Minimum to function: **office = 1 chair (throne)**, **dining room = 1 table + 1 chair**, **bedroom = bed**. (3:00:05–3:00:19)
- [BLUEPRINT] The mayor's full set also wants a **coffer, weapon rack and armor stand**, which turned the rooms "operational/satisfied". The bedroom still wanted more value: add engravings or 1–2 statues. (3:00:57–3:01:04, 3:02:29–3:02:38)
- [BLUEPRINT] Captain of the guard office (from the justice video): table, **2 cabinets**, weapon rack, armor stand. See Justice below. (5:54:58)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Queue the tables and thrones first ("do now" / raise priority) when a noble appears. (2:59:31–2:59:38, 3:01:18)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Assign the noble to each zone. Unassign their old bedroom and make sure they can reach the new rooms. The process is the same for other nobles. (2:59:52, 3:01:53–3:02:03)
- [BEHAVIOUR] **Noble demands**: about 3 items per season with roughly a 3-month window, or arrests follow. The demanded items are usually banned from export, so **never sell demanded items to traders**. Act once the demand turns red. Example: the mayor demanded 3 coffins. (2:59:12–2:59:26, 3:02:09–3:02:19, 3:02:41)
- [BEHAVIOUR] A happy mayor acts as the fort's "therapist": sad dwarves meet with them and both get good thoughts. The mayor is also the diplomat with traders. (3:00:27–3:00:45)

## Soap (3:08–3:12)
- [BLUEPRINT] Workshops needed: butcher, kitchen (renders fat into tallow, an automatic job), wood furnace (ash), ashery (needs a barrel, a bucket and blocks to build), soap maker (needs a bucket to build). A stoneworker or carpenter makes the blocks. (3:08:48–3:09:33)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Chain: butcher a fatty animal (yak is practical; an elephant gives 19–27 fat) > kitchen renders tallow automatically > wood furnace makes ash (repeat) > ashery makes lye into a bucket > soap maker makes soap from tallow + lye. 1 tallow = 1 soap bar. (3:09:56–3:12:43)
- [BLUEPRINT] Stock target: **about 50 buckets** before mass lye production. When slaughtering big animals, also have a large food stockpile and a tanner. (3:11:51–3:11:59, 3:10:33)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Soap is used at wells and other water sources for baths (good thoughts). Hospitals use it against infection. Soap from oil is possible but harder. (3:12:43, 3:12:19)

## Hospitals (4:04–4:12)
- [BLUEPRINT] Place it centrally and easy to reach, near the main staircase/entrance and the guest rooms. (4:04:56–4:05:15)
- [BLUEPRINT] Size: **fit about 10 patients (one squad)**. The example held about 6–7 beds. (4:05:15–4:05:27, 4:08:02)
- [BLUEPRINT] Furniture: **beds (6+)**, **traction bench** (table + mechanism + rope, built at the mechanic's workshop), **table** (for casts/surgery), **chest/coffer/box/bag** (stores thread, cloth, splints, crutches). Doors on the entrances, floor smoothed first. (4:06:05–4:06:32, 4:08:11–4:08:39)
- [BLUEPRINT] Stock: **about 10 crutches and about 10 splints** in the hospital (carpenter, wood is fine). Plus thread, cloth, buckets. Optional: plaster powder (casts) and soap. (4:11:39–4:11:49)
- [BLUEPRINT] The hospital needs a **well/water source** nearby for cleaning wounds. Well water beats river water. (5:17:13–5:17:24)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Meeting-area zone > "+" > Hospital. **Assign a Chief Medical Dwarf** (noble screen); the hospital doesn't work without one. The CMD does diagnosis, surgery and bone setting unless optional specialist doctors are set. (4:09:15–4:10:23, 4:10:56–4:11:16)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Supply settings: thread, cloth, splints, crutches and buckets are required ("buckets are necessary for keeping dwarves alive"). Soap and casting powder are optional. Most supplies fill automatically; powder and soap need production. Access: citizens + long-term residents. (4:10:39–4:12:20)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Thread comes from the farmer's workshop (spider webs, shearing) and cloth from looms. Keep both going. (4:11:52–4:12:12)

## Guild halls (4:12–4:17)
- [BLUEPRINT] **No required furniture.** The example used tables + thrones for seating, plus a **toy stockpile** (custom > finished goods > toys), because toys attract children. (4:12:36, 4:16:12–4:16:57)
- [BLUEPRINT] Value target: **2000** if the guild petitions for it. Raise value with wall engravings and statues. Best as its own enclosed room. (4:15:44–4:15:53, 4:17:06–4:17:27)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Zone (meeting) > "+" > Guild hall > choose the profession (miner, woodworker, carpenter, bowyer, woodcutter, stoneworker, doctor, metalworker...). (4:14:46–4:15:15)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Set access to **citizens (not members only)** so anyone, including children, can visit and skilled dwarves teach them. This trains children and spreads skills (doctors, ambush, crossbow). (4:13:30–4:13:54, 4:16:03–4:16:09)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Once enough workers share a skill (the UI shows "N workers, no established guild"), a guild forms and petitions for a hall with a value requirement. Grant it, or the guild is disappointed. (4:14:05–4:14:17, 4:15:15–4:15:28)

## Trading (4:17–4:25)
- [BLUEPRINT] Build the **trade depot early**, among the first buildings. Later caravans bring **wagons**, which need a **2-tile-wide path, no doors**. Early small carts can come through a single door, so a first depot can be fully indoors. (4:17:59–4:18:35)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Assign a **broker** (noble screen). Any dwarf works, but a skilled one gets better prices and the skill grows with use. Then press "broker requested at depot". Without a broker, any dwarf may come and prices suffer. (4:18:42–4:19:11, 4:20:06–4:20:17)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Caravans arrive at the start of a season and stay **over a month**. A "leaving soon" warning appears. Move goods early. (4:20:43–4:21:06)
- [BEHAVIOUR] **Elves: never sell wood**, including wooden items, barrels, and anything decorated with wood. Sell stone crafts (crowns, earrings, figurines) and stone mugs, which are easy to mass-produce and safe. Sell only your own crafts whose materials you know. (4:19:47–4:19:58, 4:22:11–4:22:21)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Traders want about **30% profit**, and a better broker lowers that. Animals bought from traders are already tame. Gifts raise the faction's opinion. Seizing goods angers them. Also check the weight limit. (4:23:01, 4:24:12, 4:24:25–4:24:50)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Useful buys seen from elves: caged animals, seeds, toys, instruments, splints, rope, cloth, barrels, buckets. (4:23:14–4:24:02)

## Burrows (4:25–4:27)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Burrows lock assigned dwarves into an area. Uses: siege/emergency shelter, or confining workers to specific workshops. Burrows can span several z-levels. (4:25:12–4:25:41, 4:26:03)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Burrow toggle "workshops can source materials from outside the burrow": ON for a work burrow, **OFF for an emergency burrow**. (4:26:14–4:26:35)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Emergency use: assign everyone (no select-all in the UI, so code must loop) and enable it; dwarves rush in. Pause/disable the burrow when the threat ends so they resume work. (4:26:39–4:27:28)
- [BLUEPRINT] Plan a safe shelter room that a siege burrow can cover. (4:25:26)

## Military (4:27–4:35)
- [BEHAVIOUR] A squad requires a **militia commander** first (noble screen). (4:28:18–4:28:35)
- [BEHAVIOUR] New squad: choose uniform (none/archer/metal/leather), name it, assign a leader, then fill up to **10 positions**. Prefer dwarves with weapon skills (the UI lists them) or who don't stress easily. Wrestlers are fine with no weapons. Spears and shields of any material work. (4:28:00–4:30:37)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Schedule options: off duty / staggered (every ~3 months) / **constant training, recommended at first** / ready (stay in uniform but still attend to needs). (4:30:55–4:31:40)
- [BLUEPRINT] **Barracks zone** with flags: sleep (add beds), store squad equipment and ammo (archers), **train here**. A training order requires a barracks with the training flag. (4:31:43–4:32:27, 4:34:35)
- [BLUEPRINT] Soldiers need **backpacks** (and items to fill them), so stock backpacks, waterskins/flasks and food. Also keep weapon racks and armor stands in the barracks. (4:32:47–4:32:51)
- [BEHAVIOUR] The equipment "update" button makes soldiers fetch better-quality gear. Custom uniforms can set "over clothing" and "partial matches". (4:33:07–4:33:32)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Orders: station (go to a point), patrol route (waypoints), burrow defense, training. **Never give kill orders on your own dwarves**; it triggers mental breakdowns. (4:33:47–4:34:39)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Even 1–3 soldiers are worthwhile. Make one starter-axe dwarf a soldier at once on dangerous (wild) embarks. (4:35:09–4:35:19)

## Wells (5:14–5:26)
- [BLUEPRINT] A well needs **blocks + bucket + rope + mechanism**: mechanic's workshop (mechanisms), stoneworker (blocks), carpenter (bucket; metal also works), clothier (cloth rope). Keep those stocked. (5:14:46–5:16:27, 5:17:28–5:17:37)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Purpose: dwarves only drink water when there's no alcohol and hate drinking from puddles; wells are better. **Hospitals need water to clean wounds.** Soap baths happen at wells. (5:16:54–5:17:24)
- **Type 1, surface well** (fresh, non-salt water body):
  - [BLUEPRINT] Build machines & fluids > Well next to or over fresh water. (5:16:32–5:17:41)
  - [BEHAVIOUR] Draw a **water source zone** around the well. Exclude river tiles (or floor them over) so dwarves use the well, not the river. (5:17:41–5:18:05)
  - Downside: freezes in winter and is exposed outdoors.
- **Type 2, bucket well** (underground, no aquifer; least recommended):
  - [BLUEPRINT] Dig a space, **channel 1 z down** to make a pit. (5:18:15–5:18:30)
  - [BEHAVIOUR] Zone > pit/pond over the hole, set to **pond** so dwarves fill it with buckets. At about 6/7 depth switch it to **pit** to stop the filling, then build the well over the hole. (5:18:46–5:19:47)
  - Downside: dries up and needs refills; fails if surface water freezes or during long sieges. (5:19:47–5:20:01)
- **Type 3, aquifer well** (best: infinite, never freezes):
  - [BLUEPRINT] Dig sideways or down into the aquifer, wall off the flooded side, then from **one z above** dig over the aquifer and dig a hole down into the water. A light aquifer takes about half a season to fill. Build the well over the hole. It works at about **3/7 depth or more** (Blind said "3 of 5"). (5:20:16–5:21:55)
  - [BEHAVIOUR] Mining into an aquifer can trap or drown miners. Wall it off promptly. (5:20:49–5:21:00)
- **Type 4, pump-fed reservoir well** (the only way to desalinate ocean water):
  - [BLUEPRINT] Screw pump = **pipe section + enormous corkscrew + 1 block**, 2 tiles. Channel a reservoir (optionally 2 z deep) fed by the pump's output side, channel the layer above, and put the well over the reservoir. Build walls around the pump's output end: that end counts as a wall, so water can't wash the operator back. Keep it away from cliff edges to avoid flooding. (5:22:20–5:24:34)
  - [BEHAVIOUR] Pump manually with one dwarf and stop when the reservoir is full; it fills fast. (5:24:46–5:25:05)
  - Pumping makes salt water fresh. Ocean embarks often also have an aquifer. (5:25:17–5:25:37)

## Metalworking, basic non-magma (5:33–5:39)
- [BLUEPRINT] Minimal metal block: **smelter + wood furnace + metalsmith's forge (needs an anvil)**, built from rock blocks. Next to them: a small **wood stockpile** and a small **metal ore stockpile** in front of the smelter/forge. A bars stockpile is optional. Full setup: several furnaces + 2 forges. (5:34:00–5:36:28)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Chain: wood furnace makes charcoal (repeat) > smelter smelts ore (hematite into iron bars, repeat) > forge makes weapons/armor (e.g. an iron spear). Flux stone + iron allows pig iron and then steel. Platinum nuggets go to jewelry. (5:36:38–5:38:46)

## Justice (5:53–6:05)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Nobles: **sheriff, which becomes captain of the guard** after a mayor exists. Later the hammer and dungeon master (with the mayor and higher nobles). (5:54:27–5:54:42)
- [BLUEPRINT] Captain of the guard needs an **office (required)** = table + 2 cabinets + weapon rack + armor stand. Plus bedroom and dining room (morale; meetings happen there). The hammer needs nothing special; the dungeon master needs rooms. (5:54:47–5:55:34)
- [BLUEPRINT] **Prison: 1 cage or chain (rope) per 10 dwarves** (e.g. 13 for about 130 pop). Mix cages and chains/ropes in one open area under a **Dungeon zone**. Meeting the count keeps the captain and hammer happy. Nicer cells make prisoners much happier. (5:56:26–5:57:10, 6:02:06–6:02:11)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Without a prison, accepted convictions become beatings that can kill. The **hammer** carries out beatings; give that job to a **weak dwarf with the weakest weapon** so offenders aren't killed. The captain of the guard handles reports, takes dwarves to prison and feeds prisoners. Add a dungeon master in big or crime-heavy forts. Guard squads can be appointed. (5:56:08–5:58:14, 5:57:10)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Open cases: **convict when there are witnesses** (e.g. disorderly conduct with 7 witnesses). Interrogation finds witnesses, trains the captain's speaking skill, and can expose vampires, who then get sealed in a cell. Interrogating visitors from public taverns is useful. Cold cases can still be convicted; law-valuing and related dwarves get happy thoughts. (5:59:23–6:00:14, 6:02:11–6:02:23, 6:03:10–6:04:12)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Prison often **improves** mental health (repentant/guilty thoughts), so it's acceptable to let the justice system run. Kids who bring prisoners water get good thoughts. (6:01:48–6:03:06)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Ignore the conspiracy and coup intelligence: plot resolution is unfinished (Villains part 2), and stolen artifacts are hard to recover. (5:59:10, 6:05:02–6:05:30)

## Libraries and paper (6:05–6:13)
- [BLUEPRINT] Paper chain: **quern** (stoneworker; a mill also works) with a **screw press** (needs mechanisms) right next to it, plus a **craftsdwarf workshop**. Use pig tail/cloth plants (keep a surplus farm). (6:06:34–6:07:12, 6:08:01)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Quern: mash plant into slurry > screw press: press slurry into a paper sheet > craftsdwarf: make a quire (writing material), plus book bindings (rock) and scroll rollers. Bind a book = binding + written quire. (6:07:24–6:08:48, 6:10:53–6:11:05)
- [BLUEPRINT] Library furniture: **chests** (the example had 7) for written works and quires. Tables and chairs for scholars/scribes are implied. (6:10:09–6:10:30)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Zone > "+" > New library. Access: visitors bring scholars, who speed up discoveries, but also thieves; citizens + long-term residents is safer. Set targets: written works count, **copies to scribe**, chests, writing material (quires). Spare quires make scribes copy books, which can be sold. (6:09:06–6:10:07)
- [BEHAVIOUR] Assign **scribes** (copy books; toggle "focus on library" to drop other tasks) and **scholars** (more scholars mean more discoveries and new books; young worlds produce many more). Visiting scholars help. (6:11:13–6:12:13)
